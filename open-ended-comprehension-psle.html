PSLE Open-Ended Comprehension Framework

Stop guessing and start answering with purpose. This framework breaks down every open-ended comprehension question into a clear method, covering the 4-step approach, when to lift, rephrase or infer, and all 8 core question types with real PSLE examples.

📋 2025 SEAB syllabus aligned 🎯 8 question types · Real PSLE examples

The 4-Step Answering Method

Most marks lost in open-ended comprehension are not from a lack of reading ability. They come from a lack of method. Every question, regardless of type, can be approached with the same four steps.

01
Read the Command Word
Decide whether the question is asking you to state, identify, give, quote, explain, infer, sequence or evaluate. The command word tells you exactly what kind of answer is expected and whether you should lift directly or construct a response.
02
Locate the Evidence
Use the line reference, paragraph, sentence before, sentence after, character action, speech or reaction. Never answer from memory. Go back to the text. The answer is always in the passage, or supported by it.
03
Decide the Answering Method
Lift when the answer is directly stated and fits. Rephrase when the wording must be made clearer. Infer when the answer is suggested but not stated. Choosing the wrong method is one of the most common reasons students lose marks.
04
Write and Check
Answer the exact question in a complete, concise sentence. Use the required number of points, accurate passage evidence and clear pronoun references. Re-read the question after writing to confirm you answered what was actually asked.

When to Lift, Rephrase or Infer

Which method you use depends on the command word and where the answer sits in the passage.

Direct Answer
Lift
Use when the answer is directly stated in the passage and the wording fits well. Copy exactly. Do not paraphrase unless the question says to use your own words.

The 8 Core Question Types

Every open-ended comprehension question in PSLE Paper 2 falls into one of these eight types. Once your child can spot the type, they know exactly which method to use and how to structure the answer.

Type 1 Literal / Factual Retrieval

Tests: Directly stated information in the passage.

Technique — LOCATE–SELECT–ANSWER: Find the stated detail in the passage → Select only what answers the question (do not copy too much) → Write it clearly in a complete sentence.
Real PSLE Example (2024, Paper 2, Q71): "Why was Li Mei 'eager to finish washing the dishes' (line 1)? [1m]"
Type 2 Vocabulary in Context

Tests: The meaning of a word or phrase as used in the passage — not the dictionary definition.

Technique — BEFORE–AFTER–SUBSTITUTE: Read the surrounding context → Infer the meaning from how the word is used → Substitute your answer back into the sentence to check it makes sense. Avoid dictionary meanings that do not fit the passage context.
Real PSLE Example (2024, Paper 2, Q73): "Pick out a two-word phrase that tells us Li Mei was talented in baking. [1m]"
Type 3 Inference

Tests: Meaning that is suggested but not directly stated. Students must read between the lines.

Technique — CLUE–INFER–LINK: Identify the clue in the passage → State what it suggests → Link the inference back to the question clearly. Never guess. Every inference must be grounded in passage evidence.
Real PSLE Example (2023, Paper 2, Q74): "How can you tell that the writer shared a close relationship with Aunt Lily? [1m]"
Type 4 Character / Feeling / Trait

Tests: A character's emotion, personality or inner state — usually not stated directly but shown through behaviour.

Technique — NAME–EVIDENCE–EXPLAIN: Name the precise feeling or trait (not just "sad" or "happy") → Cite the character's exact action, speech or reaction from the passage → Explain how it proves the named feeling or trait.
Real PSLE Example (2024, Paper 2, Q78a): "What does her body language tell us about how she felt?"
Type 5 Cause and Effect

Tests: The reason, result or consequence of an event, action or feeling.

Technique — IDENTIFY–TRACE–MATCH: Identify whether the question asks for the cause OR the result → Trace the link in the passage → Give only the requested side. A common mistake is giving the effect when the question asks for the cause, or vice versa.
Real PSLE Example (2024, Paper 2, Q77): "What caused 'a pang' (line 27) in Li Mei's heart? [1m]"
Type 6 Tone / Mood / Attitude

Tests: The feeling, atmosphere or attitude created by the passage or expressed by a character.

Technique — LABEL–EVIDENCE–EXPLAIN: Name the precise tone, mood or attitude → Cite a word, action, description or punctuation clue from the passage → Explain how it creates that effect. Be precise: not "sad" but "despondent"; not "happy" but "relieved" or "elated".
Real PSLE Example (2024, Paper 2, Q78c): "What does his first facial expression tell us about how he felt?"
Type 7 Main Idea / Purpose / Message

Tests: The big idea, lesson, message or the writer's purpose in writing the passage.

Technique — CENTRAL IDEA–CHANGE–MESSAGE: Identify the repeated central idea or problem in the passage → Track the key change or outcome → State a specific message or purpose supported by the passage. Do not give a moral that is too general or not linked to the actual passage content.
Real PSLE Example (2024, Paper 2, Q80): "Explain how Li Mei's view about her baking talent had changed in the story and the reason for the change. [2m]"
Type 8 Evaluative Questions

Tests: A reasoned judgement or reflection about a character's choice, behaviour, value or lesson. A question about change is evaluative only when you must judge or reflect on that change, not simply describe it.

Technique — JET (Judgement → Evidence → Tie-back):
1. State your judgement clearly: Yes, No or To some extent.
2. Select exact passage evidence — one decisive action, speech or consequence.
3. Explain why the evidence proves the judgement.
4. Answer every part of the question. Personal opinion alone does not earn the mark.
Common formats: "Do you think [character] was right to [action]? Support your answer with evidence from the passage." / "Would you have done the same? Why or why not?"

Final Answer Checklist

Before your child submits the paper, run through this checklist for every open-ended answer.

Before You Submit: Check Every Answer

  • Did I answer the exact question asked, and not a similar one?
  • Did I use evidence from the passage to support my answer?
  • Did I give the correct number of points for the marks allocated?
  • Did I lift only when the answer was factual, exact or evidence-based?
  • Did I rephrase when the copied words were unclear or too informal?
  • Did I infer only when the answer was hidden or suggested, and did I explain the inference clearly?
  • Did I use clear pronoun references so the reader knows exactly who I am referring to?
  • Is my answer written in a complete sentence?

The Final Principle

Read the question carefully. Use the strongest clue in the passage. Answer precisely. Lift exact facts, rephrase for clarity, infer only from evidence.
